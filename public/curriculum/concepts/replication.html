Replication

Leader-follower, multi-leader, quorum.

distributed-systemsdatabasesreplication

Mental model

Replication keeps copies of data on multiple nodes for durability and read scaling. Single-leader is simple but the leader is a write bottleneck; multi-leader and leaderless trade simplicity for availability. The core tension is replication lag — followers are always slightly behind.

How to study Replication

Begin by restating the mental model in your own words, then connect it to a concrete system you have built or operated. Name the mechanism, the constraint it addresses, and the trade-off it introduces. Use Designing Data-Intensive Applications to check details, but close the source before writing your explanation. Retrieval is the learning step; rereading is only preparation.

Next, compare Replication with CAP & Consistency Models, Consensus. Ask what changes in correctness, latency, resource use, operability, and failure recovery. Complete Read-your-writes under lag and preserve the command, input, output, and one failed attempt as evidence. Finish by explaining the idea without jargon to someone who has not studied the track.

Proof of understanding

  • Explain the mechanism from first principles and identify the state it reads or changes.
  • Give one situation where the concept is the right choice and one where it is not.
  • Predict a realistic failure mode before running the drill, then compare the prediction with evidence.
  • Connect the result to a roadmap or build artifact instead of treating the concept as isolated trivia.

Common mistakes

  • Reading from a follower and seeing stale data ("read your own writes" violation)
  • Assuming failover is instant and lossless
  • Ignoring replication lag in capacity and correctness planning
